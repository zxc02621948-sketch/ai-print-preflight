File & delivery tips

This tool doesn't create .ai files or print-ready PDFs for you. When you're new to printing, don't lead with a pile of pro spec terms. Just ask the print shop to confirm whether your adjusted image can be printed as-is, or to convert it to a printable format, and ask if there's an extra handling fee.

What to say (beginner-friendly)

  • "Could you help me confirm whether this image can be printed?"
  • "If it needs converting to a format you can print, please convert it and let me know the cost."
  • "If there's a problem with resolution, bleed, or trimming, just tell me what I need to change."

Vector Guide

What is vectorizing?

Vectorizing redraws a pixel-based image like PNG / JPG into lines, nodes, and color shapes. Once it works, a logo, icon, or badge can be scaled up a lot without the edges going blurry from too few pixels. But it's an advanced option, not a must for printing; in most cases it's faster to raise the resolution first and export a high-resolution PNG / PDF.

Good candidates for vectorizing

  • Clear edges, clean background.
  • Simple color blocks, roughly 8 to 16 colors.
  • Logos, icons, badges, silhouettes, or flat symbols.

Don't force these

  • Thickly painted characters, photos, smoke, lots of light and shadow.
  • Fine gradients or textures that turn into tons of color blocks.
  • When the result looks messy or the file is huge, use a high-resolution PNG / PDF instead.
Do I still need to fix resolution after vectorizing?

If you've successfully converted to a clean, pure vector SVG / PDF, that graphic itself usually doesn't need a DPI fix. If your artwork still has a raster background, photos, or textures, or you'll export as PNG in the end, come back to this tool to check the resolution.
