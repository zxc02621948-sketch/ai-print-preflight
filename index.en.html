File handoff tips

This tool doesn't produce .ai files or print-grade PDFs directly. When you're new to printing, don't lead with a pile of pro spec jargon. Just ask the shop to confirm whether your adjusted image can be printed as-is, or to convert it to a printable format for you, and ask if there's an extra handling fee.

What to say to the shop

  • "Could you help me confirm whether this image can be printed?"
  • "If it needs to be converted to a format you can print, please convert it and let me know the cost."
  • "If there's any problem with resolution, bleed, or cropping, just tell me what I need to change."

Vector Guide

What is vectorizing?

Vectorizing redraws a pixel-based bitmap like a PNG / JPG into lines, nodes, and color shapes. Once it works, a logo, icon, or badge can be scaled up huge without the edges getting blurry from too few pixels. But it's an advanced option, not a must for printing. In most cases, raising the resolution and exporting a high-res PNG / PDF is faster.

Worth considering vectorizing

  • Clear edges, clean background.
  • Simple color areas, about 8 to 16 colors.
  • Logos, icons, badges, silhouettes, or flat symbols.

Don't force a conversion

  • Heavily painted characters, photos, smoke, lots of light and shadow.
  • Fine gradients or textures turn into tons of color shapes.
  • If the result looks messy or the file gets heavy, use a high-res PNG / PDF instead.
After vectorizing, do I still need to fix resolution?

If you've already converted it to a clean, pure-vector SVG / PDF, that graphic itself usually doesn't need a DPI fix. If your artwork still has a bitmap background, photos, or texture, or you'll export as PNG in the end, come back to this tool to check the resolution.
