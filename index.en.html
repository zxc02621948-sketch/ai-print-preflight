Delivery

File-delivery tips

This tool does not directly produce .ai or print-grade PDF/X. As a beginner, do not throw a pile of pro spec jargon at the shop; attach this tool's report and simply ask the print shop to confirm whether they can convert it to a printable format, and whether there is an extra handling fee.

Beginner delivery scripts

  • "I am not a design pro; could you help confirm whether this image can be printed?"
  • "If it needs converting to a printable format, please convert it and tell me the cost."
  • "If there are resolution, bleed or trim issues, please tell me exactly what to fix."

Illustrator

  • Good for logos, icons, badges and single-page posters.
  • Confirm artboard size, bleed, and that linked images are embedded.
  • File > Save As / Save a Copy, choose Adobe PDF; pick the PDF/X preset the shop asks for.

InDesign

  • Good for multi-page layouts with lots of small text.
  • Set up bleed in document settings; extend images and background past the bleed.
  • File > Export, choose Adobe PDF (Print); under Marks and Bleeds check Crop Marks and Use Document Bleed Settings.

Acrobat Pro

  • Good for final checks, not as a main design tool.
  • Use Preflight to check PDF/X, fonts, color and image resolution.
  • If Preflight reports errors, fix the source in Illustrator / InDesign and re-export.

Vector Guide

What is vectorizing?

Vectorizing redraws a pixel-based raster image (PNG / JPG) into lines, nodes and color blocks. A successful logo, icon or badge can scale up huge without the edges going blurry from too few pixels. But it is an advanced option, not a must for printing; in most cases, raising the resolution and exporting a high-res PNG / PDF is faster.

Good candidates for vectorizing

  • Clear edges, clean background.
  • Simple color blocks, roughly within 8 to 16 colors.
  • Logos, icons, badges, silhouettes or flat symbols.

Do not force-convert

  • Heavily painted characters, photos, lots of smoke or lighting.
  • Fine gradients or textures turn into tons of color blocks.
  • If the result is messy or the file is heavy, use high-res PNG / PDF instead.
Do I still need to fix resolution after vectorizing?

If you have cleanly converted it to pure vector SVG / PDF, that icon itself usually needs no further DPI fixing. If your artwork still has a raster background, photos or textures, or you will export as PNG in the end, come back to this tool to check resolution.
